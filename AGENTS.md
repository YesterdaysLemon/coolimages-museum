## Coolimages Museum

A walkable 3D museum (Three.js r170 via CDN import map, no build step) of the images in
`~/OneDrive/Pictures/coolimages`. The look follows the 1990s *Eyewitness* TV museum: white
space, pictures floating without borders, DK-style callout labels, and travel by walking
*into* paintings (the camera rolls 90° and fades through white).

Live at https://coolimages.alirezaafshan.com/ (public, `noindex`). Code deploys through
Deploy Manager. Content (images and generated data) is published separately and never
enters Git or the Docker image.

### Run
- Local: `start-museum.cmd` rebuilds `content/` from the folder, then serves http://localhost:8173/.
  `node server.mjs` (port 8080) is the production server and applies the CSP.
- It must be served over http: textures and ES modules don't load from `file://`.
- Test URLs: `?test` enables drag-to-look and skips pointer lock; `&mute` starts silent.
  `window.museum` exposes `start()`, `teleport(room)`, `look(x, z, yaw, pitch, y?)`, `inspect()`, `back()`, `step(±1)` (next work while looking closer), `tap(x, y)`, `map()`, `travel(room)`, `walkable(x, z, y)`, `plan` (forms, doors, stairs), `portals` (every door with its position, normal and height; `arrives` is false if a door has no way out on the other side), `loading` (how many works have their full image, what's queued, door previews waiting), `audit(room?)` (works something hides from a viewer standing in front of them: rays to a 5×5 grid over each picture, with what blocks them; expect `[]`), and `tick(seconds)` / `press(code, down)` to advance the simulation without waiting for frames. A hidden browser pane throttles frames and reports a 0×0 viewport, so drive checks with `tick` and emulate a viewport for anything that uses screen coordinates. The door check: for each portal, stand about 2 m in front facing it, hold W, and expect to arrive in `dest`, standing somewhere walkable (57 doors, including the front doors both ways and the lone door outside).

### Content vs code
- `python tools/build_assets.py` writes `content/art/*` and `content/manifest.json`. Both are gitignored because they are third-party art.
- Images become WebP: `<id>.webp` (max edge 1536) and `<id>.sm.webp` (max edge 800, the manifest's `small`), lossy (quality 82) for photos and lossless for PNG sources (screenshots, pixel art). Phones and low-memory devices (`lowPower` in `src/main.js`) load the small ones, and every device caps textures (1024 on phones, 2048 otherwise). Each item also carries `color` (average colour) and `blur` (a ~24 px WebP data URI, ~300 bytes) for the loading placeholder.
- Videos (`.mp4/.mov/.m4v/.webm`) need `ffmpeg`/`ffprobe` on PATH. Each becomes `<id>.mp4` (H.264 CRF 28, max edge 960, ≤30 fps, capped at 1.2 Mbit/s, AAC 80k, faststart), `<id>.webp` (poster, the item's `file`) and `<id>.sheet.jpg` (a 3×2 contact sheet the curator sees instead of the video). Manifest items carry `video`, `sheet`, `duration` and `audio`.
- Encodes are reused until the source changes or the settings do: `IMAGE_VERSION` / `VIDEO_VERSION` in `build_assets.py` (bump to re-encode everything); the record is `content/.build-cache.json`.
- **Progressive loading** (`prepareArt` / `planLoads` in `src/main.js`): the world is built at once with every work as its blurred placeholder; the Entrance Hall's works and the banners' works load first (the Enter button waits for those, at most 12 s), then everything else, room by room outward through the doors from wherever you are (re-planned on each room change, 6 at a time, 3 on phones). A room whose works have all arrived gets its door previews re-rendered (one per frame); banners redraw when their work arrives (`world.artLoaded`).
- `python tools/publish_content.py` rebuilds and drops works listed in `content-policy.json`. It uploads over SSH (`vps-admin`) to `/srv/coolimages/releases/<stamp>`, then atomically repoints `/srv/coolimages/content`. Caddy serves that directory at `/content/`. No redeploy is needed.
- **Videos are served from Cloudflare R2**, not from the site (Cloudflare's CDN terms reserve video for its paid/developer products). Bucket `coolimages-media` (account `1791d4793f0ebf04e8a0af92fb9dbba4`), served only through the Worker `coolimages-media` (`media/worker.js`, `media/wrangler.toml`) at `https://coolimages-media.alirezaafshan.com/<id>.<sha8>.mp4`: edge-cached (Cache API, one day), byte ranges, CORS for the site and localhost. Publishing stages each video on the VPS under its content-hashed name, points the public manifest's `video` at the media host, then `POST`s `/sync`; the Worker (also on a cron, every 6 hours) copies any listed video it lacks from `https://coolimages.alirezaafshan.com/content/art/<name>` and deletes objects the public manifest no longer lists, so takedowns leave R2 too. No upload key exists anywhere; the Worker's R2 binding does the writing. Deploy Worker changes with `npx wrangler deploy` in `media/` or the Cloudflare API. The bucket has no public domain of its own; keep it that way so every read goes through the Worker.
- **The free-tier guard:** the account is on the Workers free plan, whose hard cap (100,000 requests a day) keeps R2 reads well under R2's free 10 million a month (repeat views come from the edge cache). If the Worker is over its cap or a video fails to load, the site shows "The server is super poor. Please donate to help ♥" in the video's frame, a one-time notice, and a line in the caption, all linking https://github.com/sponsors/YesterdaysLemon. `python tools/publish_content.py --skip-build --pause-videos` switches videos off on purpose (`videosPaused` in the public manifest); publish again without it to switch them back on. Storage is ~20 MB of R2's free 10 GB.
- Curated text is code-adjacent data in the repo: `data/catalog.json` (pipeline-written works, merged under the hand-written `WORKS`) and `data/layout.json` (generated wings). Both ship in the app image and are validated in CI.
- Push to `main` runs `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`: syntax checks, a server smoke test, then the signed Deploy Manager webhook (app id `coolimages`, ports 3280/3281, `/opt/coolimages/app`).

### Growth pipeline
- **PC (Windows Task Scheduler)** runs "Coolimages Museum Pipeline" every 2 days at 11:00 (`tools/install_schedule.ps1` registers it; entry point `tools/run_pipeline.ps1`, log in `logs/pipeline.log`). `tools/pipeline.py` does build, then publish. If the live site gained works, it runs `gh workflow run curate.yml` so curation starts right away.
- **GitHub Actions** (`.github/workflows/curate.yml`, daily at 21:17 UTC and on dispatch) runs `tools/curate.py`. It reads the **published** manifest and images from the live site, so withheld works never reach the API. It calls `claude-opus-5` with structured JSON output, `effort: high` and server-side refusal fallbacks (`fallbacks: "default"`, beta `server-side-fallback-2026-07-01`). It commits `data/catalog.json` (plaques and callouts) and `data/layout.json` (generated wings of 3 to 7 works, templates `salon`/`white`/`night`/`pastel`; works that don't fit are held on the Entrance Hall's acquisition easels), then dispatches `deploy.yml`.
- **Auth is Workload Identity Federation**, with no API key anywhere. The job gets a GitHub OIDC token with audience `https://api.anthropic.com`; a refresher loop keeps it fresh because tokens are single-use and last about 5 minutes. The SDK exchanges it using the repository variables `ANTHROPIC_FEDERATION_RULE_ID`, `ANTHROPIC_ORGANIZATION_ID`, `ANTHROPIC_SERVICE_ACCOUNT_ID` and optional `ANTHROPIC_WORKSPACE_ID`. These are identifiers, not secrets. The Anthropic rule `coolimages-curate` (`fdrl_01Rhtjp1ac69rHdviNWpVrPC`, service account `coolimages-curator`, Default workspace) pins the subject to `repo:YesterdaysLemon@129180138/coolimages-museum@1386815787:ref:refs/heads/main`. GitHub's `sub` now embeds the owner and repository numeric IDs, so the plain `repo:owner/repo:...` form fails with `match_subject_prefix`. The rule also requires the claims `repository_owner_id`, `repository_id`, `ref` and `job_workflow_ref` (this workflow file), `event_name` in `schedule`/`workflow_dispatch` (CEL), and audience `https://api.anthropic.com`. Denied exchanges show their reason under Console > Settings > Workload identity > history. Keep `curate.py` on a single client per run.
- **The association web.** Wings connect by two-way doors between related rooms, listed in `data/layout.json` as `links: [[a, b, "door phrase"], …]`; the phrase is the door's sign on both sides. Each original wing has one far door (its first link). Each generated wing takes up to 3 links (the attic 2; the classic box 2 when it holds more than 6 works) plus a door back to **the Stair Hall**. The Entrance Hall's far door opens onto the Stair Hall: a grand flight up to a U-shaped balcony (`layout.stairs.up`, up to 5 doors) and a flight down into a pit (`layout.stairs.down`, the basement). `planWeb()` in `src/world.js` drops links over capacity and adds Stair Hall doors (then extra links) until every wing is reachable. With no generated wings, the original wings' far doors chain gallery → eyes → familiars → bedroom → gallery. `stairhall` is a reserved key.
- **Outside** (`outside`, `buildOutside` in `src/architecture.js`): the vestibule's bronze front doors are a frameless portal whose leaves swing open as you approach (`doubleDoors`); out there is an endless salt flat at dusk under a sky with an enormous blinking eye in it. The plain wraps every 800 m (`room.wrap`; `simulate` shifts you, and every outdoor object is drawn at its nearest copy), so walking far in any direction brings the museum back out of the haze. To find: the museum's facade (the perforated building of "The Museum, Exterior", glowing from inside), a bench facing the horizon, an easel that paints the view past itself at load (`room.pleinAir`, `paintPleinAir` in `src/main.js`), a colossal stone eye that turns to watch you, an invisible sculpture behind the museum, a lone gilt door to a random room each visit, and a signpost at the edge ("leaving" one way, "entering" the other). Rooms can set their own view distance (`env.far`; outside 420 m against 140). The outside is `secret`: the museum map leaves it (and doors to it) out until you've been there.
- **The Entrance Hall** (`lobby`, `buildEntrance` in `src/architecture.js`; content chosen in `buildEntrance` in `src/world.js`) is where every visit starts (`room.start`, also where the map's travel lands). A low concrete vestibule behind bronze front doors: the front wall is perforated in a dither pattern after the building in "The Museum, Exterior", which hangs there as "you are here", with a "How to visit" card and the name carved over the arch. Then a tall colonnaded hall: a red runner down the nave, a hanging banner per wing (its accent and one of its works; the four original wings' banners hang by their doors, then up to four Stair Hall wings), a Welcome stele and a Directory stele, the hat vitrine in a shaft of light from the skylight, the four original wings' doors in the aisles, new acquisitions on easels at the far end (then on the aisle walls), and the Stair Hall door between the two bookends.
- **Architecture** (`src/architecture.js`): every generated wing has a `form`: `spiral` (a ramp around an atrium up to a landing; Guggenheim), `basilica` (white temple nave, coffered vault, gold apse), `iron` (iron-and-glass hall with rails), `crypt` (low stone vaults, candles), `octagon` (domed chapel, or a white octagon for white wings; at most 4 works), `enfilade` (three salons through arches), `attic` (rafters, roof lights, fairy lights), or the classic box: `void` (white), `cinema` (screening room) or `rect`. The curator picks the least-used form for the template when a wing is created; a missing or unfit form falls back to a stable default.
- **Floors at several heights:** each room has `room.floors` (rects, circles, ring sectors at a height `h`, rects with a `ramp` for stairs, and helices). `ground()` and `walkable()` in `src/world.js` keep you on the surface within a step (0.45 m) of where you are, so balconies can overhang halls and you can't step off an edge. Obstacles may carry a height band (`y0`, `y1`). Doors have a height (`pos.y`) and only open at your level.
- Local runs: `python tools/curate.py --dry-run` lists the queue. `--source local` reads `content/` instead of the site; it needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` or an `ant auth login` profile. Python deps: `pip install -r tools/requirements.txt`.

### Collector extension (extension/)
- A Chromium MV3 extension (works in Helium; load it unpacked from `extension/`). When a picture or video from X is saved into a folder whose path contains "coolimages" (settable in the popup), or through its "Save to coolimages" menu or popup, it writes a note `<id>.json`: the post URL, author, text, alt text, post time, save time, and `match` (`exact` = X's public embed endpoint confirms the post carries this file; `page`/`nearby` = from the page only; `none`). The file stem is the X media key, as the folder already uses.
- `content.js` reports which post you right-click or click; `background.js` resolves it (download referrer, recent posts) against `https://cdn.syndication.twimg.com/tweet-result` (no login; token = `(id / 1e15 * PI).toString(36)` without zeros and dots); `lib.js` holds the pure logic, checked by `node extension/test.mjs` (includes real posts; needs the network).
- **Native helper** (`extension/host/`): `install.ps1` registers `com.coolimages.collector` under HKCU for Helium (`Software\imput\Helium`), Chromium and Chrome, with a fixed extension ID from the manifest `key` (`host/extension-id.txt`). With it, notes and saves go straight into the coolimages folder; it only writes notes beside files already in that folder and only downloads from pbs/video.twimg.com. Without it, notes and saves go through downloads into `Downloads\coolimages-inbox`; that is silent only if the browser's "Ask where to save each file" is off (Helium has it on by default; extension downloads then prompt).
- `tools/ingest_inbox.py` (first step of `tools/pipeline.py`) moves the inbox into the folder, files notes beside their files, and withholds any work whose post asks not to be reposted (adds it to `content-policy.json`; commit that). `tools/build_assets.py` uses a note's save time instead of the file time and writes `content/sources.json`; `publish_content.py` publishes it for public works. The site shows "Saved from @handle's post" (and "Maker not confirmed" without a credit); the curator gets the post text and alt text as context. `node tools/backfill_notes.mjs` writes exact notes for works whose credit already links an X post. `python tools/test_collector.py` checks inbox → folder → manifest and the helper's handlers offline.
- **Save feedback:** every save shows a toast on the X page (drawn by `content.js` in a shadow root): saved (with the post it came from, or "probably" when not confirmed), already in coolimages, or couldn't save. The toolbar badge shows ✓ / ? / `=` (duplicate) and the popup's log marks duplicates. The popup asks the helper (`check`) whether each picture of the post is already in the folder before you save.
- **Duplicates by picture, not name:** `tools/dupes.py` fingerprints media (two 64-bit difference hashes of a greyscale thumbnail plus SHA-256; videos from a frame at 1 s via ffmpeg). Same picture = hashes ≤ 10 of 128 bits apart with aspect within 4%, or identical bytes; distinct works in the collection are ≥ 37 bits apart. Fingerprints are cached in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\CoolimagesCollector\fingerprints.json` (`COOLIMAGES_FINGERPRINTS` overrides it). The helper won't save a duplicate and moves one saved straight into the folder to `<folder>\_duplicates`; ingest moves inbox duplicates to `coolimages-inbox\duplicates`; `build_assets.py` puts one copy of each picture in the museum (the catalogued one, else the largest). In each case the other post is added to the kept file's note as `alsoPosted`. `python tools/dupes.py` lists duplicate groups in the folder.

### Notes lab (private)
- `lab/index.html` is a private claude.ai artifact where the collector writes notes per work and drafts plaque text with Claude (`sample` capability). Notes live in the artifact's `db` store (`notes/<id>`: `text`, `updatedAt`, `draft`, `approved`), read-locked to the owner and editors by db rules. Raw notes must never be committed: this repo is public.
- Build its data with `python tools/lab_export.py` (writes the gitignored `lab/works.json` and `lab/thumbs/`), then republish the artifact with those files. Withheld works get an entry but no image.
- "Sync my lab" means: read `notes` with ArtifactData, copy each `approved` `{title, note}` into `data/overrides.json`, commit and deploy, then re-export and republish the lab so those works show as live. `src/main.js` applies overrides over both the hand-written and the generated catalogue.

### Layout
- `src/catalog.js` holds the hand-written curatorial data: wings, titles, notes and callouts (`{u, v, t}` in image space), keyed by filename stem.
- `src/credits.js` holds verified attributions (`creator`, `handle`, `profileUrl`, `sourceUrl`, `license`, `confidence`) plus the removal-request URL. Research with evidence is in `research/attributions.json`: high and medium confidence are credited, low is not. Key claims were re-checked against the creators' own posts.
- `src/world.js` builds the rooms. Rooms sit ~200 m apart in one scene; only the current room is visible, and every room has exactly 1 hemisphere light + 2 point lights so shader programs are shared. Collision uses each room's floors minus its obstacles (`circle`/`rect`/`sector`). Door previews render once at load into a shared multisampled target and are copied into small per-door textures.
- `src/textures.js` holds the procedural canvas textures, plaques, wall text, notices and callout decals.
- `src/audio.js` is the generative Web Audio score (a mood per wing), footsteps and the portal whoosh.
- `src/main.js` holds the renderer, controls, inspect mode, portal transitions, the museum map, the credits panel, HUD and minimap. Controls: pointer lock with a drag-look fallback; arrow keys turn; on touch the left thumb is a joystick and the right thumb looks. A tap (or a click without pointer lock) acts where it lands (`tapAt`): a picture to look closer, a door to walk through, the floor to walk to. While looking closer, ←/→, A/D, a swipe or the caption buttons step to the next work in the room. Tab, the minimap or the menu opens the museum map (floors as bands); rooms you've visited (kept in localStorage) can be revisited from it. Portrait screens widen the field of view (`fovFor`).
- `server.mjs` is the static server: `/healthz`, a strict CSP (the inline import map is allowed by hash, computed at startup after CRLF normalization), `X-Robots-Tag: noindex`, and an allowlist of served paths.

### Decisions
- The public site stays public but unindexed. Every work carries credit where traced plus a removal-request link (GitHub issues). Works in `content-policy.json` are never uploaded; a "Not shown online" card takes their place. `HS-zGlbbEAAgsIJ` is excluded because its watermark forbids reuploading and AI training. `HS_hSswasAAIOeB` is excluded because its artist's profile says "DON'T re-upload my art". Apply the same rule to any new work whose creator forbids reposting.
- Caching: images are WebP under `/content/art/` (Caddy's `@art` matcher covers any extension). A Cloudflare Cache Rule for this hostname (zone `alirezaafshan.com`, phase `http_request_cache_settings`) respects origin headers. Without it Cloudflare rewrites `no-cache` to a 4-hour browser TTL and mixes old and new JS modules after a deploy. HTML, JS and JSON are `no-cache`. Existing images are `public, max-age=86400` (Caddy `@art` + `file` matcher, so 404s are not long-cached).
- Takedowns: add the ID to `content-policy.json`, run `python tools/publish_content.py` (its sync removes a video from R2 too), then purge `https://coolimages.alirezaafshan.com/content/art/<ID>.webp` and `<ID>.sm.webp` (and a video's `https://coolimages-media.alirezaafshan.com/<ID>.<sha8>.mp4`) in Cloudflare. Otherwise edges can serve the old copy for up to a day.
- Deploy Manager's `docker run` has no volume mounts, so content is served by Caddy from the host rather than from the container.
- Images in the folder that aren't in `WORKS` or the generated catalog appear under "New acquisitions" at the far end of the Entrance Hall: four easels, then the free aisle walls (newest first).
- Portal paintings show render-to-texture previews made once at load; `renderer.compile` then warms every room.
- Callout labels (the DK-style points of interest) are hidden by default and fade in on the work you're looking at; never while looking closer. L or the menu cycles "on the work you look at" / "always on" / "off" (kept in localStorage). `TX.calloutDecal` measures labels first: margins fit the text and clear the frame border (`gap`), wrapped labels are split evenly, and the canvas grows so nothing is cut off. The label layer sits in front of the deepest frame, and the labels of the work you're looking at draw over everything (`depthTest` off until they fade), so walls, columns and frames can't clip them.
- How works are shown is mixed per wing (`PALETTES` / `displayModes` in `src/architecture.js`, `hang()`): on the wall, on an easel, leaning against the wall, on wires from a rail, floating, or on a freestanding panel. Floor-standing works stand in front of their wall spot. The spiral and the cinema stay on the walls. `easel()` in `src/world.js` is shared: the work rests on the ledge in front of the legs and the clamp grips its top edge.
- Videos show their poster until their room is entered, then loop muted (`syncVideos` in `src/main.js`; only the current room's videos load or play). They load with `crossOrigin = 'anonymous'` (they come from the media host); the CSP's `media-src` allows it. Looking closer (E) unmutes a video with audio and ducks the music, unless sound is off (M). The `screening` template (dark velvet room, bezel screens with light spill, benches) is meant for videos, but videos can hang in any template. `server.mjs` answers byte ranges locally; Caddy does in production.
